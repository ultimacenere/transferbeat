Inter's comeback show: 3-2 win over Napoli, Lautaro decides

TransferBeat Newsroom · edited by Pierluigi Cella · Updated on 6 Sep 2026

Inter turn it around against Napoli from 0-2 to 3-2 at San Siro: five second-half goals and Lautaro nets the winner in the 92nd. The Nerazzurri go top on maximum points; Allegri's Napoli stay on three.

It was the match that lit up the third round of Serie A. At San Siro, Inter beat Napoli 3-2, overturning a game the visitors led 2-0: according to ANSA, the Nerazzurri completed the comeback late on, with Lautaro Martínez netting the 3-2 in the 92nd minute.

The scoreline tells of a night split in two. It was 0-0 at the break; all five goals came in the second half (Serie A competition data). The quiet first half and the madness of a half-hour after the restart are the heart of the San Siro clash.

Afterwards Inter head coach Cristian Chivu hailed the spectacle: «Inter-Napoli is an advert for Italian football», his words as reported by ANSA. On the other bench, Massimiliano Allegri did not hide his disappointment: «A fine contest with Inter, but you cannot concede three goals in half an hour», he said according to Corriere dello Sport.

In the table Inter climb to nine points, three wins from three, with eight goals scored and three conceded: they are second only on goal difference behind Roma, also on a perfect record. Napoli, by contrast, stay on three points — one win and two defeats, five goals scored and five conceded — and slip to tenth after three rounds.

The cover man is Lautaro Martínez, praised by team-mate Marcus Thuram: «Lautaro is our leader, winning like this is too good», the France forward told ANSA. On the Napoli side heavier talk is circulating, with some outlets calling it one of the club's most difficult league starts in years: readings to treat with caution, not confirmed by primary sources.

Now the calendar allows no rest. The third round continues with another big match, Juventus-Milan this evening between two sides on maximum points, while Inter can eye the summit backed by a group able to turn around even the toughest games. For Allegri's Napoli, meanwhile, a quick response is needed after a harder-than-expected start.
