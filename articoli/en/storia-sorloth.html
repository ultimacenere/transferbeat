Sørloth-Juventus: player agreement in place, clubs now negotiate

TransferBeat Newsroom · Updated on 8 Jun 2026

La Gazzetta dello Sport has confirmed that Alexander Sørloth and Juventus have reached a personal agreement, with Spalletti already mapping out three tactical roles for the Norwegian. The stumbling block is now club-to-club with Atlético Madrid, as Premier League sides circle.

8 June marks the day the Sørloth-Juventus deal shifted into a higher gear. La Gazzetta dello Sport published two articles within hours of each other: the first, at 05:21 UTC, confirming the personal agreement between the striker and Juventus; the second, at 06:58, revealing that Spalletti has already drawn up at least three tactical setups to accommodate the Norwegian — as a lone striker, in a two-man attack, or inside a front three. That tactical detail is significant: it signals Spalletti sees Sørloth as a first-choice starter.

The path to this point moved fast. Sky Sport reported initial contact with Sørloth's camp on 4 June; by 5 June, Eurosport and Calciomercato.com were adding that the player was fully on board and that Atlético Madrid had their eye on Nico González as a makeweight. The key moment came on 6 June when Tuttosport broke the story of Spalletti's personal phone call to Sørloth and the Norwegian's positive reply, with Calciomercato.com confirming Atlético were now willing to negotiate.

7 June brought a complication: La Gazzetta flagged concrete Premier League interest in Sørloth, a development that hands Atlético Madrid a potential auction. The Spanish club has not closed the door to English clubs, which adds urgency to Juventus's timeline.

Juventus are pushing to close the deal before the World Cup kicks off — a global showcase that could drive Sørloth's market value higher. Having the player's word already is a genuine edge: Sørloth has committed verbally to Spalletti and shows no inclination to reverse that, but the clubs still need to align on the economics.

The deal structure remains the main hurdle. Nico González, owned by Juventus, is the name most commonly floated as a part-exchange to reduce the cash outlay. Estimates put Sørloth's value at €35–40 million, though Atlético could push higher given the English competition. Direct talks between the two clubs are expected imminently.

With Vlahović set to leave Turin, the starting centre-forward role is vacant. Sørloth — 28 years old, 18 Liga goals last season — ticks every box on Spalletti's list: physicality, hold-up play, big-game goals. If Juventus can see off the Premier League competition and agree terms with Atlético, their marquee summer signing is already decided.
