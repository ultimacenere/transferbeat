Dibu Martinez-Juve, the keeper says yes: Villa now want €10m

TransferBeat Newsroom · Updated on 23 Jun 2026

Sky Sport and Eurosport have for weeks confirmed the agreement between Juventus and Emiliano 'Dibu' Martínez: the Argentine goalkeeper has said yes to the Bianconeri. To get it done, Aston Villa still need convincing — they now ask €10m according to Calciomercato.com, down from the €15m Gazzetta cited early on — while Juve work to free up the spot by moving Di Gregorio on.

The trail started quietly at the beginning of June: Sky Sport listed Emiliano 'Dibu' Martínez, the Aston Villa goalkeeper, among Juventus' hot files, alongside the one for striker Sorloth. Eurosport explained why 'Dibu' could be the right goalkeeper for the Bianconeri's future.

The acceleration came on 9 June. Oggi Sport Notizie reported ongoing contacts between the Juventus hierarchy and the player's entourage; that evening La Gazzetta dello Sport went big — 'Martinez to Juve can now be done' — with Aston Villa asking €15m and the Bianconeri working on a discount.

The breakthrough came from Sky Sport, in the night between 9 and 10 June: agreement reached between Juventus and Dibu Martinez. Eurosport confirmed the deal with the goalkeeper, moving the dossier to the clubs' table; SNAI and Planetwin365 echoed the Argentine's 'yes', with a note on what was still missing to close.

From there the deal has not been plain sailing. Sky Sport returned to the topic — 'Juve-Dibu, what's missing?' — and on 12 June La Gazzetta dello Sport, in its 'Il Blitz' episode, analysed what could complicate it: the sticking point remains the financial agreement with Aston Villa.

On the outgoings front, according to Sportmediaset, Italiano is 'calling' Di Gregorio to Besiktas and Juve are setting the price for their own keeper: a sale that would free up the spot for Dibu. In parallel, the Sorloth trail for the attack also remains in the background.

The latest update comes from Calciomercato.com: Aston Villa would now ask €10m to let Dibu go, a figure lower than the €15m circulating earlier in the month. The current state of play, then: terms with the player confirmed, club-to-club talks still open but with the gap on the fee narrowing, plus Di Gregorio's exit to sort out. Meanwhile Gazzetta dedicated a profile to Dibu as a 'mad and brilliant goalkeeper' — the penalty trash talking, the work with a psychologist, ten years of grind before his breakthrough.
